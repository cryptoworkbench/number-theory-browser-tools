## Locked decisions (orchestrator; non-negotiable)

- L-CODES: Sanskrit is code `sa`, switcher label `संस्कृतम्`; Latin is code `la`, switcher label `Latina`. Both are appended after ckb in that order: SUPPORTED_LANGS, SWITCHER_OPTIONS, the page switchers and every data file (sa then la are the last two language blocks everywhere). Both are plain two-letter codes, matched exactly and case-sensitively like every other code (valid() unchanged). Both are left to right: not in RTL_LANGS, no isolates, `<html lang="sa">` / `<html lang="la">` with no dir attribute.
- L-SCRIPT-SA: Devanagari, reusing Hindi's script rule (SCRIPT_RULES.sa mirrors SCRIPT_RULES.hi: own = a Devanagari letter, the same foreign scripts, latin = SCRIPT_LATIN_NOTATION). Sanskrit additionally uses virama-final words (भवेत्, संस्कृतम्), the avagraha ऽ, the visarga ः and the vocalic ऋ ॠ ऌ (ॡ allowed too). The repertoire is GATE SA_CPS: anusvara, visarga, the vowels अ–ऌ, ए ऐ ओ औ, ॠ ॡ, the consonants क–ह without ऩ ऱ ळ ऴ, the avagraha, the vowel signs ा–ॄ, े ै ो ौ, ॢ ॣ and the virama; the dandas । ॥ are allowed. Everything else of Devanagari is rejected by the new permanent finding SA-LETTER: nukta and nukta letters (Hindi/Urdu loan sounds), candra and short e/o (ऍ ऑ ॅ ॉ ऎ ऒ ॆ ॊ), the candrabindu ँ, ळ, ॐ, the Vedic accents and the abbreviation sign ॰ (Devanagari digits stay NATIVE-DIGIT). The danda । and double danda ॥ are sentence punctuation in prose only, never inside a formula: the new permanent finding SA-DANDA rejects a danda preceded by whitespace or followed by anything but whitespace, a placeholder or the end of the value.
- L-SCRIPT-LA: Latin script written without macrons (as Vicipaedia and most modern Latin texts do): ASCII letters only; any other letter only when the English value has it (Bézout's é); φ always allowed (the zgh/ku precedent). u/v distinguished as in modern usage, i/j not: consonantal u is written v (vel, videt, valor; GATE LA-UV), consonantal i is written i and j is never used (iam, maior, eius). ae/oe are two letters, never æ/œ. New permanent findings LA-LETTER (the letters) and LA-J (a word with j or J that is not in the English value).
- L-PLURAL: Intl.PluralRules has no sa or la data (measured: Node 22.23.1 / ICU 78.2 / CLDR 48, supportedLocalesOf gives [] for both; they resolve to en-US, and under LANG=ru_RU to ru-RU, selecting many for 0 and few for 2). So both join the engine's FIXED_PLURAL_LANGS and are never passed to Intl. Latin is { one, other }: one for exactly 1, other otherwise. Sanskrit has a grammatical dual and is { one, two, other }: one for exactly 1, two for exactly 2, other otherwise (the shape Hebrew uses, here fixed by the engine): the new internal FIXED_DUAL_LANGS = ['sa'] in the engine, mirrored in the checker, where expectedPluralCategories adds two for it. The selection is the same with the real Intl, with a Russian-like Intl and with no Intl, under any default locale.
- L-DIGITS: ASCII digits only, literally the English numerals (comma grouping, dot decimal) in both; never Devanagari digits ०–९ (NATIVE-DIGIT), never Roman numerals in place of digits (DIGIT-PARITY; GATE LA-ROMAN). Both join DIGIT_PARITY_LANGS.
- L-DETECT: no new detection branch. Both resolve through the existing two-letter fallback: sa, sa-IN, sa-Deva, san (ISO 639-2 Sanskrit) and every case/separator variant give sa; la, la-VA, la-IT, la-Latn, lat (ISO 639-2 Latin) give la. Collisions of the fallback, measured during planning (GATE engine) and recorded like the kok/jam/kum precedents (T-qz1-02): sat (Santali), sah (Yakut), sad (Sandawe), sag (Sango), sas (Sasak), saq (Samburu) now default to Sanskrit; lad (Ladino), lag (Langi), lah (Lahnda), lam (Lamba), lav (the ISO 639-2 code of Latvian; browsers send lv, which stays lv) and a bare Latn script subtag now default to Latin. The Latn case is the one existing i18n-check.js detection assertion whose expected value changes (en -> la); a new Cyrl,en -> en case keeps its intent (a script subtag alone is not a language).
- L-FONT: system fallback only: no font link, @font-face or font-family change (a locked project rule; do not raise it). The test machine has Droid Sans Devanagari, FreeSans, Noto Sans Devanagari and Noto Serif Devanagari covering the whole Sanskrit repertoire (GATE font); Latin is ASCII and covered by the existing webfonts.
- L-TRANSLATE: two separate translations from English (never one derived from the other, never from another language's block, never from the Hindi values): Sanskrit in standard Classical Sanskrit prose with modern scholarly and technical neologisms where needed, using the traditional Indian mathematical terms where they exist (लब्धि quotient, शेष remainder, भाजक divisor, गुणक multiplier, भाज्य dividend, कुट्टक for the Āryabhaṭa pulverizer if the extended Euclidean algorithm needs a gloss) while staying comprehensible; Latin in the Neo-Latin scientific register (numerus primus, divisor communis maximus, modulus, congruentia, inter se primi, radix primitiva). The five playback buttons are action nouns (D-COMMON). Validation states the requirement with भवेत् ("N 2 तः 1000 पर्यन्तं पूर्णाङ्कः भवेत्।"); status banners use past participles (प्राप्ताः found, समाप्तम् done, गणितम् computed, रचिताः built). Alice and Eve are feminine, Bob masculine (प्रेषितवती / प्रेषितवान्).
- D-REGISTER-LA: second person singular imperative (tu): elige choose/pick, inscribe enter/type, preme click/press, adde add, aufer remove, dele delete, purga clear, computa compute, ostende show, cela hide, adhibe use, trahe drag, specta watch, tempta try, genera generate, exsequere run, deprome download, rescinde undo, repete redo, fortuito elige randomize. Validation "… esse debet" (N inter 2 et 1000 esse debet.); status banners in the perfect passive (inventi sunt, factum est, computatum est). Alice, Bob and Eve stay indeclinable; agreeing adjectives and participles are feminine for Alice and Eve, masculine for Bob.
- D-ORTHO-SA: class nasal with virama inside a word before a stop, never anusvara (सङ्ख्या, खण्डः, अन्तः, सम्बन्धः; GATE SA-ANUSVARA), anusvara before semivowels, sibilants and h (संयोगः, संशयः); word-final m before a consonant-initial Devanagari word is anusvara (इदं पश्यतु; GATE SA-FINAL-M), before a vowel, a Latin token, a numeral, a placeholder or at the end it stays म्. Light sandhi (the modern simple-Sanskrit style): no vowel or consonant sandhi between separate words, visarga kept before every word; full internal sandhi inside compounds (तत्समकावयवः, द्व्याधारिक). Never a nukta, candra vowel or candrabindu (SA-LETTER) and never a Hindi grammar word (है, की, को, में, से, ने, और, नहीं, लिए, करें …; GATE HINDI-WORD). NFC, no zero-width character.
- D-COMPOUND-SA: compounds are closed (गुणनखण्डवृक्षः), except that a foreign name joins its Sanskrit head noun with a hyphen (यूक्लिड-कलनविधिः, वेन-आरेखः). In buttons, chips, legends, table headers and labels prefer a two-word phrase (genitive or adjective) to a long compound; no word without a space or hyphen may exceed 26 code points (GATE SA-LONGWORD); the 375px layout check is the final arbiter.
- D-ORTHO-LA: no macrons, no ligatures, no accents (LA-LETTER); v for consonantal u (LA-UV), never j (LA-J); a capital only for the first word of a sentence or label, proper names and adjectives formed from names (Euclideus, Cayleiana, Fermatiana, Vennianum, Shorianus, Sinicum); Neo-Latin technical words take regular declension (computatrum, -i; navigatrum, -i; bitus, -i).
- D-PUNCT-SA: the danda । (U+0964, written as the literal character) ends every prose sentence where English ends with a full stop, directly after the last word, placeholder or closing bracket, followed by a space, a placeholder ({notes}) or the end of the value (SA-DANDA); a value never ends with "." and "." never follows a Devanagari letter (GATE SA-FULLSTOP); "." stays inside numerals and formulas; ॥ is accepted by every rule but not used in UI text. `? ! : ; ,` as in English with no space before them.
