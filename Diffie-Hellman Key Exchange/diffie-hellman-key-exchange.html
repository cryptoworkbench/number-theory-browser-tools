Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

Watch Alice and Bob agree on a shared secret in full view of an eavesdropper — nothing that crosses the wire is secret, yet only the two of them can compute what they land on.

•

How this works

Alice and Bob agree in the open on a prime modulus p and a generator g. Each of them privately picks a secret exponent and uses it to compute a public value from p and g — then they trade only those public values across a wire that Eve is tapping. Each side raises the public value it received to its own private exponent, and remarkably, they both land on the exact same number: a shared secret Eve cannot compute from what she intercepted, even though she saw everything that crossed the wire.

This is a teaching demo: toy-sized parameters, private exponents drawn with Math.random (not a cryptographic random source), and the raw shared secret is displayed here rather than run through a key-derivation function. Never reuse anything on this page for anything real.
