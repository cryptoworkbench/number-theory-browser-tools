Euclidean Algorithm

Repeatedly replace the pair (a, b) with (b, a mod b) — divide the larger by the smaller and keep only the remainder — and the pair shrinks every step. The moment one side reaches zero, the other side is the greatest common divisor of the two numbers you started with.

The same GCD can also be seen as the primes the two numbers share →

brisk
Set a and b, then press Run.

Each line's s and t express that line's remainder as a combination of the two original inputs — s·A + t·B = r.

Consecutive Fibonacci numbers are the worst case for this algorithm — they force the most division steps for their size.
