Square and Multiply

This is what a computer really does when it evaluates be mod m — no repeated multiplication, just one squaring per bit of the exponent and an occasional multiply by the base.

•

How the computer actually does it

Raising a number to the e-th power by multiplying it by itself e times is hopeless once e has hundreds of digits — a real RSA exponent would take longer than the age of the universe that way. Instead, the machine reads the exponent in binary and needs only about one squaring per bit, with an extra multiply thrown in only on the bits that are 1. That is the whole trick, and it is the only reason cryptographic-sized arithmetic finishes at all.

This is the identical loop the RSA and Diffie-Hellman pages on this site call every single time they write be mod m — there it is named modular exponentiation and runs behind the scenes; here it is opened up, one bit at a time.
