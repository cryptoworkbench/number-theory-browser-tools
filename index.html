Number theory, visualized

Fifteen small browser tools for exploring primes, factorization, modular arithmetic, simultaneous congruences, group tables and isomorphisms, and public-key cryptography from modular arithmetic to elliptic curves — each one an animated, hands-on visualizer you can nudge, drag a slider on, and watch work.

Primes and divisibility

Sieve of Eratosthenes

Give every natural number its own box, then watch the sieve strike out everything that isn't prime — with playback controls and a chime for every prime found.

Factor Tree

Hand any number a factor tree and watch it grow, branch by branch, down to its prime leaves.

Venn Diagram

Drag primes into two or three overlapping circles and read off each region's product — every overlap reads out as an intersection, from a simple ∩ lens up to the centre where all the circles meet.

Euclidean Algorithm

Divide the larger number by the smaller, keep only the remainder, and repeat — watch the pair shrink step by step in a growing equation chain until nothing is left over but the greatest common divisor.

Modular arithmetic and groups

Chinese Remainder Theorem

Give the same unknown number two or three remainder facts — leftover 2 when divided by 3, leftover 3 when divided by 5 — and watch those evenly-spaced families of candidates cross at the single number that satisfies all of them at once.

The Equivalence Wheel

Every natural number belongs to exactly one equivalence class modulo N — arranged here as concentric rings and wedges, one wedge per class.

Euler's Totient Function

Count how many numbers below n share no factor with n — not with a formula, but by running the Euclidean algorithm against every single one of them and tallying who survives.

Cayley Table

A group's entire behavior fits in one square table — set a modulus, flip between addition and multiplication, and click any cell to see its identity, its self-inverse elements, and its symmetry all sitting visibly in the grid's shape.

Group Isomorphisms

Two side-by-side wheels — the integers mod n under addition, the units mod m under multiplication — for every pair where they are, structurally, the exact same group. Click either wheel to see the correspondence on the other.

Cryptography and factoring

Square and Multiply

The machine never multiplies a number by itself a billion times — it reads the exponent in binary and squares its way down, one squaring per bit, which is the only reason RSA-sized arithmetic finishes at all.

Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

Watch Alice and Bob build a shared secret in the open while Eve records every byte that crosses the wire — and still can't reconstruct it.

Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman

The whole point set of a small elliptic curve over a prime field, plotted as a scatter — Alice and Bob walk across it by scalar multiplication and land on the same point from opposite directions.

RSA

Watch Bob and Alice build RSA keypairs, trade them over a wire Eve is tapping, and see exactly what hard math problem keeps her locked out.

Fermat's Method

Fermat's factoring method: search for a² − N = b², then watch the algebra turn into a picture as a square gets rearranged into a rectangle.

Shor's Algorithm

Watch the algorithm that makes RSA mortal factor a number stage by stage — with the one genuinely quantum step, order finding, honestly labelled as a classical stand-in, which is why N stays small here.

All fifteen tools run entirely client-side in your browser — no build step, no server, no tracking.
